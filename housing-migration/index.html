An investigative case study

Housing age and recent migration in Madrid

Did Carabanchel’s unusually concentrated 1960s housing stock create more capacity for a recent migration wave? This page tests a plausible timing story against all 21 districts. It does not claim that old buildings cause migration.

The proposition

A coincidence of housing turnover and migration

The hypothesis is that a large wave of homes completed in the 1960s may now be reaching a common turnover period as original occupants age, die, or move. If that turnover coincided with Madrid’s recent migration wave, it could have made more homes available in Carabanchel at the right moment.

The available data can test the timing and scale of the housing stock, and whether recent foreign-born population growth is associated with it. It cannot observe the proposed household mechanism. The citywide chart is therefore as important for counterexamples as for supporting evidence.

How to read this

Sources, calculation and limits

Buildings and weighting

Catastro building records are kept when currentUse is residential and the construction year is valid. numberOfDwellings is the weight. A record with zero dwellings still counts in the building line but contributes zero to dwelling-weighted measures.

Boundary matching

Buildings are assigned by a point on their surface to current 2026 census sections. The 2025 foreign-born share is attached to that geography. A 2021–2025 section change is published only where the older and newer section match reciprocally with at least 95% overlap.

District change

District population values do not inherit section crosswalk uncertainty: 2021 and 2025 INE birthplace counts are aggregated directly by district code. The citywide comparison uses the share of recorded residential dwellings built from 1961 through 1970.

Interpretation

Dwellings represent housing capacity, not occupied households. Foreign-born residents are not synonymous with recent arrivals. Correlation here cannot distinguish housing supply, affordability, networks, tenure, location or many other mechanisms.

What these data do not identify

Owners, tenure, deaths, moves to care homes, vacancies, rents, household turnover, dwelling occupancy, or individual migration decisions. Those would require additional evidence before the proposed mechanism could be tested directly.
